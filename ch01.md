## concept Definitions of economics | 6
- * Adam Smith (Wealth of Nations, 1776) - economics as a science of wealth: how it is produced, exchanged, distributed and consumed.
  - Production = combining land, labour, capital and entrepreneur; distribution = paying factor owners their rewards; consumption = the final purpose of wealth.
- * Alfred Marshall (Principles of Economics, 1890) - study of mankind in the ordinary business of life; a social science, wealth central but the real concern is welfare.
- * def Robbins' definition (1932) :: Economics studies human behaviour as a relationship between many ends (unlimited wants) and scarce means that can be put to alternative uses - the scarcity-and-choice view.
- eg Alternative uses: one plot of land could grow crops, hold a factory or house a family - the owner must choose.
- tip Order of the three: Smith = wealth, Marshall = welfare, Robbins = scarcity and choice.

## compare Positive vs normative economics | 7
| Point | Positive economics | Normative economics |
| * Question asked | What is? (facts) | What ought to be? (judgements) |
| Content | Tested, accepted cause-and-effect facts | Suggests the right or good course and solutions |
| Example | Unemployment raises poverty and lowers living standards | Government should create jobs to cut unemployment |
- * A positive statement can be checked and shown right or wrong; a normative one carries ethical norms and value judgements.
- ! Watch for the words "should", "must", "ought" in an MCQ stem - they signal normative.

## compare Microeconomics vs macroeconomics | 8-9
| Point | Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
| * Focus | Individual units - a household, a firm, one price, one industry | The economy as a whole |
| Key topics | Market demand and supply, consumer theory, production theory, costs | National income, inflation, unemployment, interest rate, public debt |
| Scope | Commodity pricing, factor pricing (rent, wages, interest, profit) - "price theory", welfare, what / how / for whom | Growth and development, national income determination, inflation and employment, BOP and trade, macro policies |
| Uses | Resource allocation, price mechanism, input to policy, tax planning via elasticity | Understanding complex systems, hitting growth targets, price stability, fixing BOP deficits |
- def Boulding on micro :: Micro studies a particular firm, household, individual price, wage, income, industry or commodity.
- * The two are interdependent: a single firm's hiring and investment decisions depend on how healthy the overall economy is.
- ! Self-test traps: theory of unemployment is NOT micro; theory of firm behaviour is NOT macro.

## compare Needs, agents, factors of production and rewards | 10-11
- Need = essential for survival (food, an inhaler for an asthma patient); want = nice to have, life goes on without it (heavy bike, chocolate).
- * Agents (participants): households (consume and supply labour), firms (produce, allocate factors to maximise profit), government (allocates tax revenue among social needs), foreign traders (exchange across economies).
- ! "Students" is not one of the listed agents.
| Factor | Type | Nature | Reward |
| Land | Non-human, natural - gift of nature (minerals, water, sunlight, climate) | Passive | Rent |
| Labour | Human - mental or physical effort for money | Active - combines the others | Wages |
| Capital | Non-human, man-made wealth used to create more wealth (machines, roads, schools) | - | Interest |
| * Entrepreneur | Human - organises the other three and bears risk and uncertainty | Decision maker | Profit (or loss) |
- def Rent (Samuelson) :: Payment for using a factor whose supply is fixed - in economics only the part of a tenant's payment that is for the land itself.
- ! Air is not a factor of production; interest rewards capital; land is the non-human factor; demand for land is derived demand (self-test answer).

## concept Types of goods | 11-12
- * def Merit goods :: Socially desirable goods with positive externalities that the market tends to under-produce and under-consume - education, health, parks.
- * def Demerit goods :: Goods seen as harmful to society, creating negative externalities - e.g. alcohol.
- * def Public goods :: Non-rival and non-excludable - nobody can be kept from using them - national defence, highways.
- def Private goods :: Rival and excludable - one person's bread cannot be eaten by another.
- def Club goods :: Excludable but non-rival until congestion sets in - private golf courses, club services.
- * By income: normal goods (demand rises with income, income elasticity positive), inferior goods (demand falls as income rises, negative - public transport, second-hand items), superior / luxury goods (income elasticity above 1 - luxury car).
- x "Inferior" refers to affordability, not to poor quality.

## concept Division of labour and capital formation | 12-13
- * Division of labour = splitting production into tasks done by different workers instead of one person doing all.
- eg Sports suits: one worker doing every stage makes about 5 a day; four workers each on a specialised stage make about 30 a day.
- Why output rises: learning by doing (less training, lower average cost), time saved (no switching between tasks), specialisation, and regional / international specialisation (Pakistan and cotton - comparative advantage).
- * def Capital formation :: Net addition to a country's stock of man-made capital goods over time, made by giving up part of current consumption.
- * Three stages: create savings (depends on power and will to save) -> mobilise savings through the capital market -> invest them in real capital.
- x Consumption and capital formation move in opposite directions.

## concept Scarcity and opportunity cost | 13-14
- * def Scarcity :: Resources (land, labour, capital, entrepreneur) are never enough to satisfy all wants of individuals and government.
- * def Opportunity cost :: The next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
- eg Student: the extra hour of sleep or outing with friends forgone is the cost of attending a lecture.
- eg Government: food output lost as farmland becomes housing societies.
- Households forgo satisfaction from good B; firms forgo revenue from supplying good B; governments forgo social need B (e.g. health instead of education).
- ! Opportunity cost to society of a new school = the other goods the same resources could have produced, not the money spent.

## formula Production possibility frontier | 15-17
- * def PPF :: The set of output combinations of two goods an economy can produce when all its scarce resources are used fully and efficiently.
- Concepts: trade-off, choices, efficient vs inefficient use (e.g. COVID-19 under-use), growth, increasing opportunity cost.
- Assumptions: efficient use, full employment, fixed resources, two goods only, constant technology.
- f Opportunity cost of X in terms of Y :: (Y2 - Y1) / (X2 - X1) :: X = good gained (guns, 10 thousands); Y = good given up (bread, 100 thousand pounds) :: A to B: (90 - 100) / (1 - 0) = 10 bread per extra gun unit; B to C: (70 - 90) / 1 = 20; C to D: (40 - 70) / 1 = 30; D to E: (0 - 40) / 1 = 40 -> rising cost :: Sign is negative (trade-off); quote the size of the sacrifice
| Combination | Guns (10 thousands) | Bread (100 thousand lb) | Bread given up |
| A | 0 | 100 | - |
| B | 1 | 90 | 10 |
| C | 2 | 70 | 20 |
| D | 3 | 40 | 30 |
| E | 4 | 0 | 40 |
- * Properties: slopes down left to right (trade-off) and is concave to the origin (opportunity cost rises).
- * Why concave: law of diminishing returns and resources not equally suited to both goods (rice land switched to cotton gives little cotton for much rice).
- * Point on the curve = efficient; inside (M) = unemployed or inefficient resources; outside (N) = unattainable now.
- * Growth (more or better resources, e.g. investing in doctors, engineers, CAs) shifts the PPF outwards (AE to A'E'), making N attainable.
- ! Improved labour skills shift the PPF out; cutting unemployment only moves from inside the curve onto it. Equity is not shown by a PPF.

## compare Economic systems - capitalism, socialism, mixed | 20-23
- * Central questions: what to produce, how to produce, for whom to produce.
- Circular flow (two sectors): households supply factors to firms and receive factor prices; firms sell goods and services to households and receive product prices.
- def Economic system :: The set of principles a society uses to answer the central questions, covering production, distribution and efficient use of land, labour and capital.
- * No pure capitalism or socialism exists; real economies are mixed - mixed capitalist if market features dominate, mixed socialist if state features dominate.
- Free market (laissez-faire): decisions by the free play of market forces - text lists Canada, Chile, Germany, Japan, South Korea.
- Command (centrally planned): decisions by a central planning body - North Korea, Cuba, Finland.
- Mixed: market forces plus central planning - Sweden, France, UK, USA, China, Netherlands.
| Point | Capitalism | Socialism | Mixed economy |
| * Ownership | Private individuals own and manage resources | State owns major means of production | Public and private sectors co-exist |
| Prices | Price mechanism - free demand and supply | Set by government | Market prices plus government correction of imperfections |
| Competition | Yes - free entry and exit | None; no profit motive | Yes, with state controls |
| Motive | Self-interest - profit, rent, wages, utility | Social welfare; state provides basic needs | Personal freedom plus social welfare (minimum wage, support prices) |
| Government role | Law, contracts, peace and security only | Central planning of production, consumption, distribution | Infrastructure, key industries (defence, energy), reduce inequality |
- def Laissez-faire :: French for "leave alone" - an 18th-century idea that government should not interfere with market forces (no fuel price control, no minimum wage).
- def Mixed economy (Samuelson) :: An economy in which public and private sectors cooperate.
- * Capitalistic mixed economy: private sector uses the factors, state ensures growth without concentrating economic power. Socialistic mixed economy: state owns basic and strategic industries and dominates major decisions.
- ! Free price mechanism, competition and self-interest = capitalism; "no cartels" is NOT a demerit of socialism.

## concept Islamic economic system | 24-25
- * Features: Allah as sustainer (earn by halal, fair means); God is the true owner, humans are trustees; state ownership allowed alongside a free market; moderation ("middle way"); no riba; earnings only from permitted goods; hoarding discouraged; zakat on the wealthy for the poor.
| Point | Capitalism | Islamic system |
| * Distribution | Full liberty, large disparities | Balanced via zakat, sadaqat, bequest |
| Exploitation | Common - weak agents exploited | Curbed - usury, gambling, speculation banned |
| Interest | Banks justify it as a service charge | Replaced by Murabaha, Musharakah etc. |
| Monopoly | Cartels earn high profit | Public-interest businesses jointly owned, state steps in |
| Ownership | Unrestricted private ownership | Nationalisation supported to stop wealth concentration |
| Freedom | Unconstrained profit seeking | Allowed within halal / haram limits |
- * Sharia law turns these principles into law: no making money from money, wealth only from legitimate assets and trade, no investment in gambling, tobacco or alcohol, no short selling or non-asset-backed derivatives.
- Islamic finance shares risk: bank and customer split profit or loss; the bank charges no handling fee unless it earns the customer a profit.
- def Mudaraba :: Specialist investment managed by a financial expert, with profits shared between bank and customer.
- def Musharaka :: Investment partnership - profit shares fixed in advance, loss limited to capital invested.
- def Murabaha :: Interest-free credit - the bank buys the item and resells it to the customer on deferred payment.
- def Ijara :: Lease - the bank buys an item and leases it to the customer for an agreed period, earning rent.
- def Ijara-wa-Iqtina :: Ijara where the customer can buy the item at the end of the contract.

## exam How this chapter is tested in the CBE | 6-36
- ! Mostly one-mark MCQs: match a definition to its author and book (Robbins - Nature and Significance of Economic Science, 1932), spot positive vs normative statements, place a topic in micro or macro.
- ! PPF MCQs: read a point as efficient / inefficient / unattainable, pick what shifts the curve outward, explain concavity (increasing opportunity cost).
- ! Numeric entry: compute opportunity cost between two PPF combinations with (Y2 - Y1) / (X2 - X1); state it as units of Y per unit of X.
- ! Classification items: factor -> reward, good -> merit / demerit / public / private / club / normal / inferior, feature -> economic system, product -> Islamic finance mode (Ijara vs Murabaha is a favourite).

## mistakes Common MCQ traps | 7-25
- x Calling "government should..." a positive statement.
- x Thinking lower unemployment shifts the PPF outward - it only moves the economy onto the curve.
- x Mixing up Ijara (lease) with Murabaha (buy and resell on deferred terms) or Ijara-wa-Iqtina (lease with purchase at the end).
- x Calling public transport inferior because it is low quality - it is about income, not quality.
- x Treating club goods as non-excludable - they exclude non-members.

## recall Quick recall | 6-27
- Smith = wealth; Marshall = welfare; Robbins = ends, scarce means, alternative uses.
- Positive = what is; normative = what ought to be.
- Micro = individual units; macro = whole economy.
- Factors and rewards: land-rent, labour-wages, capital-interest, entrepreneur-profit.
- Labour active, land passive; entrepreneur bears risk.
- Merit = positive externality; demerit = harmful.
- Public = non-rival, non-excludable; private = rival, excludable; club = excludable, non-rival until congested.
- Normal (+), inferior (-), superior (> 1) income elasticity.
- Capital formation: create, mobilise, invest savings.
- Opportunity cost = next best alternative forgone.
- PPF: downward sloping, concave, rising opportunity cost; growth shifts it out.
- Central questions: what, how, for whom.
- Capitalism = price mechanism, private property, self-interest; socialism = state ownership, planning.
- Islamic: no riba, zakat, trusteeship, halal earnings; Mudaraba, Musharaka, Murabaha, Ijara, Ijara-wa-Iqtina.
