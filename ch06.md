## concept Keynesian setting | 173
- * Keynes's income and employment theory rests on consumption, saving and investment; equilibrium national income is where C + I = C + S, i.e. planned investment equals saving.
- Saving and investment together drive growth and development; rational households do not spend all income but set some aside for future plans.

## formula Consumption function | 174-175
- * def Consumption :: The part of disposable income that households spend on durable and non-durable consumer goods.
- Poorer families spend a bigger share of income on survival than richer families; spending patterns differ between income groups.
- * f Consumption function :: C = Co + cY :: Co = autonomous consumption (independent of income); cY = induced consumption; c = slope = MPC :: Co 50, c 0.5, Y 300 -> C = 50 + 150 = 200 :: Co is the y-intercept, not the slope
- def Autonomous consumption :: The basic spending a household must make whatever its income (food, clothing, healthcare); at zero income it is financed from past savings or borrowing.
- def Induced consumption :: The part of consumption that varies with income - more comforts and luxuries as income rises.
- * f Marginal propensity to consume :: MPC = change in C / change in Y :: Share of EXTRA income spent :: Income +10,000, spending +7,000 -> MPC = 0.7 :: Do not use total C / total Y
- * f Average propensity to consume :: APC = C / Y :: Share of TOTAL income spent :: Y 400, C 250 -> APC = 0.625 :: APC falls as income rises while MPC stays 0.5 in the book table

## rule Psychological law of consumption | 175-176
- * Consumption rises when income rises, but by less than the rise in income.
- Propositions: aggregate C grows by less than aggregate Y (once basic needs are met, extra income is saved); whatever is not consumed is saved; higher income raises both consumption and saving.
- * Hence 0 < MPC < 1 (strictly - never 0 and never 1).
| Y | C = 50 + 0.5Y | MPC | APC |
| 0 | 50 | - | - |
| * 100 | 100 | 0.5 | 1.00 |
| 200 | 150 | 0.5 | 0.75 |
| 300 | 200 | 0.5 | 0.67 |
| 400 | 250 | 0.5 | 0.625 |
| 500 | 300 | 0.5 | 0.60 |
- * On the graph the 45-degree line marks C = Y; below the crossing point Y* consumption exceeds income (dissaving), above Y* consumption is less than income.
- tip In the table Y* = 100: C equals income, so saving is zero there.

## concept Determinants and shifts of the consumption function | 176-177
- * Real income is the most important determinant (it moves consumption ALONG the curve).
- Other determinants shift the curve: distribution of wealth (more equal -> more consumption, since the rich have a lower propensity to consume); expected price rises (spend sooner); fiscal policy (heavier tax on low-income households cuts C); interest rates (higher rates raise loan repayments and cut C); consumer confidence.
- * Upward shift C1 -> C2 = more spending at every income level, e.g. optimistic forecasts.
- eg Expected rise in GST or cut in subsidies -> people buy now -> consumption function shifts up (self-test answer).

## formula Saving function and propensities to save | 177-179
- * def Saving :: Income not yet consumed - consumption postponed by households or businesses.
- * f Saving identity and function :: Y = C + S, so S = Y - C; S = -So + sY :: So = dissaving at zero income (equals Co); s = MPS :: Y 0: S = 0 - 50 = -50; Y 500: S = 500 - 300 = 200 :: Saving is negative at low income, not zero
- * f MPS and APS :: MPS = change in S / change in Y; APS = S / Y :: MPS = share of extra income saved; APS = share of total income saved :: Y 400, S 150 -> APS = 0.375; MPS 0.5 throughout :: APS rises with income in the table (0, 0.25, 0.33, 0.375, 0.4)
- * f MPC + MPS = 1 :: Since change in Y = change in C + change in S, divide by change in Y :: Income +10,000: spent 7,000 (MPC 0.7), saved 3,000 (MPS 0.3); 0.7 + 0.3 = 1 :: Also 0 < MPS < 1; APC + APS = 1 too (e.g. 0.625 + 0.375)
- eg From C = 500 + 0.75Y: MPS = 1 - 0.75 = 0.25. From C = 80 + 0.5Y: MPC = MPS = 0.5. From S = -60 + 0.3Y: MPS 0.3, MPC 0.7, so MPC > MPS.

## concept Determinants of saving | 179
- * Level of income: saving rises with income.
- Net wealth (assets less debts): a fall in net wealth makes people spend less and save more at each income.
- Interest rate: a higher rate rewards saving and discourages credit buying, so saving rises.
- Objective and institutional factors: political stability, secure property and a developed banking system encourage saving; heavy indirect taxes push people to spend to keep living standards, cutting saving (saving curve shifts left).
- Subjective motives (Keynes): providing for the future, a reserve for contingencies, business projects and expansion, prudent debt repayment.
- def Hoarding :: Income neither consumed nor deposited but kept at home - a leakage (self-test term).

## concept Investment and the investment function | 180-181
- * def Investment :: Spending by households or firms on new capital goods - buildings, roads and communications, plant and machinery.
- * x Buying an existing asset is only a change of ownership: Zain pays Ijaz Rs 10m for an old workshop - Zain's spending is cancelled by Ijaz's disinvestment, so the country's capital stock is unchanged.
- * f Investment function :: I = Io + mY :: Io = autonomous investment; m = marginal propensity to invest; Y = national income :: --
- * def Autonomous investment :: Investment that does not change with national income, often government welfare spending (infrastructure, schools, hospitals); a flat line, e.g. Rs 100m at every income from 100 to 500.
  - Shifts (Io -> I*o) with exogenous changes such as big public development programmes.
- * def Induced investment :: Investment that varies with national income, motivated by profit; an upward-sloping line, e.g. rising from Rs 10m to Rs 50m as income goes from Rs 100m to Rs 500m.
  - Shifts (I1 -> I2) with interest rates or social factors.

## formula Marginal efficiency of capital and the interest rate | 181-183
- * Keynes: the two determinants of investment are the MEC (expected rate of return) and the market rate of interest; invest only if MEC exceeds the interest rate.
- def Marginal efficiency of capital :: The discount rate at which the present value of an asset's expected yields just equals its supply price - in practice, the expected return on new investment.
- def Market rate of interest :: The price, as a percentage of principal, of using funds now that are repaid later.
- * f Investment decision :: Gain = (MEC - interest rate) x amount invested :: MEC = expected return; interest = borrowing cost or return forgone on deposits :: Zain Rs 100,000 at 6% expected, interest 4% -> gain 2% = Rs 2,000; if interest rises to 8% -> loss 2% = Rs 2,000 :: Same logic with own funds - the deposit rate is the opportunity cost
- * MEC schedule slopes down: lower interest rates bring more investment (movement along the curve, a -> b).
  - Reasons: more investment raises demand for and the supply price of capital goods; more output lowers product prices.
- * In Keynes's short run the interest rate is taken as constant, so MEC mainly decides investment.
- MEC shifts (not a movement) when non-interest factors change:
  - Right: optimism, higher incomes (e.g. tax concessions), fast population growth, cost-reducing technology.
  - Left: rising wage rates, many existing capital goods / substitutes, taxes on inputs, cuts in investment subsidies.

## process Government means of influencing investment | 183
- * Keep interest rates low to encourage investment (higher rates cut it).
- Direct encouragement: investment grants (possibly regional), lower cost of doing business, rule of law, tax incentives.
- Build business confidence with a stable, consistent growth policy - sudden policy changes destroy confidence.
- Support technology: finance research by itself and private firms; education for long-run R&D.
- Influence consumption (e.g. money supply and credit control), which affects investment through the accelerator.
- Government infrastructure spending creates demand that draws in private investment.

## mistakes Common traps | 174-183
- x Computing MPC as total C / total Y (that is APC).
- x Allowing MPC to equal 0 or 1; Keynes's law requires strictly between.
- x Treating purchase of a second-hand asset as investment.
- x Calling a change in the interest rate a shift of the MEC curve (it is a movement along it).
- x Forgetting that saving is negative (dissaving) below the break-even income.
- x Adding MPC and APS (only MPC + MPS = 1 and APC + APS = 1).

## recall Quick recall | 173-184
- Equilibrium: C + I = C + S, so I = S.
- C = Co + cY; Co autonomous, cY induced.
- MPC = change C / change Y; APC = C / Y.
- Psychological law: C rises by less than Y; 0 < MPC < 1.
- Y = C + S; S = -So + sY.
- MPC + MPS = 1.
- Real income is the main determinant of C.
- Saving rises with income, interest and stability.
- Investment = spending on new capital goods.
- I = Io + mY; autonomous flat, induced upward.
- Invest when MEC > interest rate.
- MEC curve slopes down; shifts with expectations, wages, technology, taxes.
- Government: low rates, grants, confidence, R&D, infrastructure.
