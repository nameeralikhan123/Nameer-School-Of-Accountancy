## concept The multiplier idea | 194
- * Core logic: one person's spending becomes another person's income, so an injection of investment keeps circulating in shrinking rounds.
- def Investment multiplier (kI) :: In a simple closed economy with only consumption and investment, the number you multiply a change in autonomous investment by to get the final change in total output - i.e. delta Y divided by delta I.
- * Only exogenous (autonomous) investment - investment determined outside the model - is used.
- eg Output rises by Rs 1,000 bn after extra investment of Rs 500 bn -> k = 1,000 / 500 = 2; output rise of Rs 2,000 bn on the same Rs 500 bn -> k = 4.
- eg Government spends Rs 100 bn on a motorway: wages, materials and machinery rent become household income; households spend part of it, which becomes income of suppliers, who spend part again - each round smaller than the last.

## formula Multiplier formulas | 194-195
- f Investment multiplier :: k = delta Y / delta I, so delta Y = k x delta I :: delta Y = change in national output; delta I = change in autonomous investment :: Output up 1,000 on investment of 500 -> k = 2 :: Use only the autonomous change in investment
- f Multiplier from MPC :: k = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS :: MPC = marginal propensity to consume; MPS = 1 - MPC :: Investment 1,000, MPC 0.5: rounds 1,000 + 500 + 250 + ... = 1,000 x 1 / (1 - 0.5) = 2,000 :: Do not divide by MPC itself
- eg Same 1,000 injection with MPC 0.8 -> k = 1 / 0.2 = 5 -> output rises by 5,000.
- * Higher MPC = bigger multiplier (direct relationship); higher MPS = smaller multiplier (inverse).
- tip Bigger leak, smaller kick: whatever leaks out of the spending chain (saving, tax, imports) goes in the denominator.
- * Works both ways: a positive multiplier magnifies a rise in investment; a negative multiplier magnifies a fall into a larger drop in output.

## rule Multiplier assumptions and limitations | 195-196
- * MPC stays constant - any change alters the final output effect.
- * Spending (investment) is continuous.
- * Price level does not change - price changes would alter consumption patterns.
- * Economy is below full employment - with no spare capacity, extra government investment mainly causes inflation and the real effect shrinks.
- * Tax rate stays unchanged - tax changes alter disposable income, so stable fiscal policy is needed.
- Limitations:
  - x Full employment: once reached, the multiplier process stops working.
  - x Consumer goods must be available in surplus - if goods are short, extra income cannot be spent.
  - x Time lags: the full effect arrives well after the initial investment.
  - def Time lag :: The delay between an economic action and its consequence - here the gap between the initial investment and the moment its full multiplier effect is felt.

## formula Multiplier extensions - tax and import | 196-197
- * Leakages: saving (consumption postponed), taxes (income taken by government) and, in an open economy, imports (spending that leaves the domestic economy).
- def Marginal propensity to tax (MPT) :: The share of extra income that goes to the government as tax.
- def Marginal propensity to import (MPM) :: The share of extra income spent on goods and services from outside the domestic economy.
- f Tax multiplier (closed economy with government) :: kt = 1 / (MPS + MPT) :: MPS = share saved; MPT = share taxed :: Save 30%, tax 20% -> 1 / (0.3 + 0.2) = 1 / 0.5 = 2 :: Adding tax makes the multiplier smaller than 1 / MPS
- f Import multiplier (open economy) :: km = 1 / (MPS + MPT + MPM) :: MPM = share spent on imports :: Save 30%, tax 20%, import 10% -> 1 / 0.6 = 1.67 :: Exports are NOT in the formula
- * Each extra leakage lowers the multiplier: 1 / MPS > kt > km for the same MPS.
- eg Sticky note: the tax multiplier measures how a country's total output responds to a change in government tax.

## concept Accelerator principle (beta) | 198
- * Introduced by J.M. Clark; demand for capital goods is a derived demand - it rises because demand for consumer goods rises.
- * A change in consumer demand leads to a corresponding but magnified change in investment in capital equipment.
- def Accelerator (beta) :: The numerical coefficient linking an increase in income (output) to the resulting increase in investment.
- f Accelerator :: delta I = beta x delta Y, so beta = delta I / delta Y (I = f(Y)) :: delta I = induced net investment; delta Y = change in output; beta equals the capital-output ratio :: COR 3, output rises 20,000 -> net investment 3 x 20,000 = 60,000 :: Multiply the CHANGE in output, not the level
- def Capital-output ratio (COR) :: The amount of capital needed to produce one unit of output.
- Assumptions:
  - * Constant capital-output ratio.
  - * Existing plants already at full capacity, so extra demand needs new plants (with spare capacity, output can rise without new investment).
  - * Surplus resources are available to build more plant and equipment.
  - * Production is flexible and firms respond quickly to demand changes.
  - * Credit is elastic - funds for induced investment are readily available; a funds shortage raises interest rates and restricts induced investment.

## example Accelerator - furniture illustration | 198-199
- * COR 3: dining sets worth 100,000 need 5 plants worth 300,000 (each plant 60,000); one plant wears out each year -> replacement 60,000 a year.
- f Gross investment :: Gross = net (new) investment + replacement (depreciation) :: Net = COR x change in output; replacement = 60,000 :: Demand 100,000 -> 120,000: capital needed 360,000, net 60,000 + replacement 60,000 = gross 120,000 :: A 20% rise in demand doubled gross investment (60,000 -> 120,000, +100%)
| Year | Demand (000) | Capital needed (000) | Replacement (000) | Net investment (000) | Gross investment (000) |
| t | 100 | 300 | - | - | - |
| t+1 | 100 | 300 | 60 | 0 | 60 |
| * t+2 | 130 | 390 | 60 | 90 | 150 |
| t+3 | 150 | 450 | 60 | 60 | 120 |
| t+4 | 160 | 480 | 60 | 30 | 90 |
| t+5 | 160 | 480 | 60 | 0 | 60 |
| t+6 | 150 | 450 | 60 | -30 | 30 |
| * t+7 | 120 | 360 | 60 | -90 | -30 |
- * Investment depends on the RATE of change of output: growth slowing from +20 to +10 already cuts gross investment (120 -> 90), even though output is still rising.
- eg Depreciation is straight line at 20% a year (one of five plants replaced yearly).

## rule Limitations of the accelerator | 199
- x Ignores adjustment costs and time lags of installing new capital - firms may move to the desired stock gradually.
- x Spare capacity means firms need not raise net investment so much.
- x Works only below full employment - at full employment labour and capital are not available.
- x A constant COR is unrealistic in practice.
- x Needs a lasting rise in consumer demand - if firms think it is temporary they stretch existing plant instead of investing.

## process Multiplier-accelerator interaction | 200
- * Samuelson: cyclical fluctuations over time come from the interaction of multiplier and accelerator.
- * 1 Autonomous investment rises (delta I).
- * 2 Income rises through the multiplier: delta Y = k x delta I.
- * 3 Higher output induces more investment through the accelerator: delta I = beta x delta Y - and the loop repeats, so growth feeds itself.
- * 4 Growth hits the economy's capacity limit; desired output exceeds what can be produced, so firms cut investment.
- * 5 Lower investment cuts output via the reverse multiplier, which the accelerator magnifies again -> sharp fall.
- * Multiplier and accelerator always work in the same direction.

## compare Business cycle phases | 201-202
- def Business cycle :: Recurring fluctuations of an economy's output over a long period, moving through distinct phases; underlying trend of real GDP is usually upward, so each cycle ends higher.
| Feature | Peak (boom) | Downturn (recession) | Trough (depression) | Recovery (revival) |
| * Output and employment | Rising to the maximum possible | Slowing, steady decline, job losses | Very low output, high unemployment | Steady rise in output and incomes |
| Credit | Banks lend freely | Banks cut credit | Low investment | Banks increase credit |
| Prices | Inflationary pressure; central bank raises interest rates | No price rise expected - deflation is a recessionary indicator | Profits and prices keep falling | Demand starts to rise |
| Confidence | Business optimism high | Firms cancel orders, scale back plans | Extremely low; machines idle | Rising; postponed spending resumes |
- tip Order: Peak -> Recession -> Trough -> Recovery (four phases); "inflation" is not one of them.

## exam How PRC tests this chapter | 194-211
- ! Numeric entry: compute k from MPC, MPS, MPT or MPM; then delta Y = k x delta I. Memorise 1/(1-MPC), 1/MPS, 1/(MPS+MPT), 1/(MPS+MPT+MPM).
- ! From a function: C = 500 + 0.75Y -> MPC 0.75 -> k = 4; S = -500 + 0.5Y -> MPS 0.5 -> k = 2. The intercept is irrelevant.
- ! Leakage wording: "of each extra Rs 1, 30% tax, 10% imports, 40% on domestic goods" - the rest (20%) is saving, so leakages = 0.6 and k = 1.67. Out of every extra 100: tax 25, imports 10, saved 15 -> k = 1 / 0.5 = 2.
- ! With MPC and MPT given (e.g. MPC 0.7, MPT 0.2), the self-test takes MPS = 1 - MPC = 0.3 -> k = 1 / 0.5 = 2.
- ! Direction MCQs: higher MPC or steeper consumption function -> bigger k; higher MPS, steeper saving function, higher taxes, near full employment, low confidence -> smaller k; k lies between 1 and infinity.
- ! Accelerator tables: net = COR x change in output; gross = net + replacement; gross investment = net investment + depreciation / replacement / capital consumption (all the same thing).
- ! Business cycle MCQs: match a description to its phase; trade cycle has four phases; deflation is recessionary; a growing economy brings inflationary pressure.

## mistakes Common mistakes | 194-202
- x Using k = 1 / MPC instead of 1 / (1 - MPC).
- x Including the marginal propensity to export in the open-economy multiplier.
- x Forgetting to deduce saving when a question gives only tax, imports and domestic spending shares.
- x Applying COR to the level of output instead of the change in output for net investment.
- x Forgetting replacement investment when asked for gross investment.
- x Calling "inflation" a business cycle phase.

## recall Quick recall | 194-204
- Spending of one = income of another.
- k = delta Y / delta I.
- k = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS.
- MPC 0.5 -> k 2; MPC 0.8 -> k 5.
- Tax multiplier = 1 / (MPS + MPT).
- Import multiplier = 1 / (MPS + MPT + MPM).
- More leakage -> smaller multiplier.
- Multiplier fails at full employment, goods shortage, time lags.
- Accelerator (Clark): delta I = beta x delta Y; capital demand is derived.
- beta = capital-output ratio.
- Gross investment = net + replacement.
- Investment follows the rate of change of output.
- Samuelson: multiplier + accelerator interaction = business cycles.
- Phases: peak, recession, trough, recovery.
