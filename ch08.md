## concept Public finance - scope and comparison | 214-215
- def Public finance :: The branch of economics dealing with how the state raises revenue (mainly taxes) and spends it on its functions - i.e. the income and expenditure of public authorities and how the two are adjusted, aiming to maximise social welfare.
- * Public income and public expenditure run side by side but need not be equal.
- * Four segments of scope:
  - Public expenditure - objectives and kinds of government spending.
  - Public revenue (income) - tax and non-tax sources, canons and types of tax.
  - Public debt - arises when spending exceeds revenue so the government borrows; covers need, sources, impact and management.
  - Financial administration - making and implementing economic policy.
- tip Scope = "RED-A": Revenue, Expenditure, Debt, Administration. Private spending is NOT a component.

| Point | Public finance | Private finance |
| * Adjustment | Decides expenditure first, then raises income | Starts from income, then fits expenditure |
| Budgeting | Annual budget compulsory | Annual budget optional |
| Borrowing | Can borrow internally and externally | Only external borrowing |
| * Objective | Social welfare | Profit |
| Secrecy | Accountable to the public | Secrecy is a personal matter |

## concept Fiscal policy and its tools | 216-217
- def Fiscal policy :: Deliberate government action using taxes and government spending to stabilise the economy and reach social, economic and to some extent political goals.
- * Tool 1 - changes in government revenue:
  - Tax revenue: income tax, property tax, wealth tax, gain tax, sales tax.
  - Non-tax revenue: interest and dividends, fees and penalties, rent on government property, royalties, trading profits of state entities, post office income, civil administration receipts.
- * Tool 2 - changes in government expenditure:
  - Development: roads, communication, agriculture, railways, utilities, irrigation, dams.
  - Non-development: defence, social services, law and order, general administration, debt servicing, subsidies.
- x A change in bank rate is a monetary, not fiscal, instrument.

## compare Expansionary vs contractionary fiscal policy | 217-218
| Point | Expansionary (anti-deflationary) | Contractionary (anti-inflationary) |
| * Measures | Cut taxes and / or raise government spending | Raise taxes and / or cut government spending |
| Problem tackled | Equilibrium below potential GDP - recession, unemployment | Equilibrium beyond potential GDP - high inflation |
| AD curve | Shifts right (AD1 to AD2); price level rises to P2 at potential GDP Yp | Shifts left (AD1 to AD2); price level falls to P2 at Yp |
| Aim | Speed up growth | Slow down growth |
- eg Self-test: actual output ya below full employment yf -> increase government expenditure.

## rule Objectives and limitations of fiscal policy | 219
- * Objectives: low inflation (stable prices), high employment, steady economic growth, equilibrium in balance of payments (imports = exports in value), balanced budget (spending can be paid for without mid-year mini budgets).
- * Limitations:
  - x Forecasting - policy depends on predictions that cannot be made accurately.
  - x Time lag - gap between the need for action and the impact being felt.
  - x Crowding out - government spending shrinks the private sector.
  - x Negative impact of tax - higher taxes may reduce work effort, productivity and aggregate supply.
  - x Poor coordination with monetary policy can make fiscal policy fail.
- def Crowding out :: The shrinking of private sector activity caused by a rise in government spending.
- eg Government spends Rs 100 bn financed by heavy borrowing -> market interest rate rises -> projects cost more -> private investors hold back, offsetting the stimulus.

## rule Functions and canons of taxation | 220-221
- * Fiscal - main source of money for development and non-development spending.
- * Allocation - fairer income distribution, e.g. progressive tax narrows gaps between income groups.
- * Regulatory - demand management by changing tax base and rates.
- * Incentive - special arrangements or tax cuts to reward or attract private investors.

- * Qualities a good tax system needs (administrative side); main four from Adam Smith.
- def Canon of equality :: Each person should pay in line with ability to pay (revenue enjoyed under state protection), so the burden is fair - the most important canon; e.g. higher income-tax rates for higher earners.
- def Canon of certainty :: Amount, time and manner of payment must be clear, not arbitrary, to the payer - and the government must know its likely receipts for budgeting.
- def Canon of convenience :: Tax should be collected when and how it is easiest for the payer, e.g. land tax with rent, consumer taxes inside the price.
- def Canon of economy :: Collection cost should be small, so nearly everything taken from people actually reaches the treasury.
- * Other canons:
  - Fiscal adequacy - taxes must cover government expenses; a small tax raising a lot beats a heavy tax raising little.
  - Flexibility - system adjusts to changing conditions (auto-adjusting aids stability).
  - Simplicity - easy for all to understand, or corruption and oppression by tax gatherers follow.
  - Diversity - a wide mix of direct and indirect taxes across industry, agriculture, services and trade.
- ! "Same rate for everybody" is NOT a feature of a good tax system.

## compare Direct vs indirect tax | 221-223
- * Classified by ability to shift the burden: impact = initial burden; incidence = ultimate burden.
| Point | Direct tax | Indirect tax |
| * Burden | Cannot be shifted - impact and incidence on the same person | Can be shifted - impact and incidence on different persons |
| Collection | Directly from the person taxed | Added to product prices (tax on expenditure) |
| Examples | Income tax, wealth tax, capital gains tax | GST / sales tax, VAT, excise duty |
| Advantages | Equitable, economical to collect, relatively certain, elastic, anti-inflationary (cuts disposable income, AD shifts left) | Corrects externalities (health tax on cigarettes), hard to evade, consumer choice, wide coverage incl. those exempt from direct tax, curbs demerit goods |
| Disadvantages | Easy to evade, unpopular, discourages saving and investment, less incentive to work | Regressive, cost-push inflation, black markets, uncertain revenue in recession, distorts markets |
- f Direct tax payable :: Tax = income x tax rate :: Burden stays with the earner :: Mr Zain earns Rs 1,000,000 at 2% -> Rs 20,000 paid to the treasury :: He cannot pass it on to anyone
- eg GST of 16% on medicines: companies include it in the price, so the end user bears it.
- * Indirect taxes raise costs, so they shift aggregate supply to the left.

## compare Progressive, proportional and regressive tax | 223-224
- * Classified by rate of tax relative to the tax base (taxable income or asset).
| Point | Progressive | Proportional (flat) | Regressive |
| * Rate vs base | Rate rises as base rises | Rate fixed whatever the base | Burden falls more heavily on the poor - inverse to ability |
| Examples | Income tax, property tax, wealth tax | Income tax within one slab, zakat, withholding tax | GST, VAT |
| For | Built-in stabiliser, equitable, certain slabs, anti-inflationary | Unambiguous, no discrimination, does not hurt work or saving | - |
| Against | Discourages activity and capital formation, encourages evasion, seen as invading rights, revenue loss through evasion | Fails canon of equality, relatively inelastic (raises less), widens inequality | Causes income inequality |
- eg 17% GST on food: everyone pays 17% per unit, but the poor feel the burden more relative to income.
- tip Progressive = poor pay less %; Proportional = same %; Regressive = poor carry more burden.

## concept Economic growth and Rostow's stages | 225
- def Economic growth :: A long-term rise in economic activity; per Friedman, expansion of the system in one or more dimensions without changing its structure - shown by an outward shift of the production possibility curve.
- * Differs from the business cycle, which is the short-run rise and fall of real GDP around the trend.
- * Rostow's five stages:
  - 1 Traditional society - primitive, agricultural, no technology.
  - 2 Pre-takeoff - shift from traditional to modern farming, limited mechanisation.
  - 3 Takeoff - industrial revolution; many still in agriculture but it matters less.
  - 4 Drive to maturity - technology spreads, industry diversifies.
  - 5 Age of mass consumption - leading sectors make consumer durables; high per-capita income and living standards.

| Indicator | Timing | Use |
| * Leading | Ahead of the cycle | Forecasts whether a peak or trough will come in the next 3-12 months |
| Coincident | Same time as the peak or trough | Government judges the current stage |
| Lagging | 3-12 months after events | Confirms past trends |
- * Classifying the cycle stage is never exact, so a mix of indicators is used.

## compare Costs and benefits of growth | 226
| Benefits | Costs |
| Higher living standards (higher real income) | Environmental damage - air, noise, water pollution (negative externalities) |
| More employment as capacity grows | Income inequality - gains go to a few |
| Fiscal benefit - more tax collected | Demand-pull inflation risk |
| Less poverty | Social cost - longer working hours, less health and leisure |
| - | Current account deficit from importing machinery and chemicals |

## exam How PRC tests this chapter | 214-236
- ! Classification MCQs: direct (income, wealth, capital gains, personal income tax) vs indirect (sales, GST, VAT, excise); progressive vs proportional (income tax in a single slab) vs regressive (GST).
- ! Policy direction: expansionary = more spending / lower tax, AD right; contractionary = less spending / higher tax, AD left. To close a gap below full employment, raise government expenditure.
- ! Definition matching: crowding out, public debt (spending exceeds revenue), budget deficit (expenditure minus receipts), balanced budget (expenditure equals tax revenue).
- ! Canon recognition: given a paraphrase of a canon, name it; ability to pay = equality, best shown by personal income tax.
- ! "NOT" questions: bank rate is not fiscal; private spending is not public finance; inflation is not a cycle phase; same rate for all is not a good-tax feature; inequality is not a government aim.
- ! Simple numeric entry: tax = base x rate (Rs 1,000,000 x 2% = Rs 20,000).

## mistakes Common mistakes | 216-226
- x Treating bank rate or interest rate changes as fiscal tools.
- x Calling GST progressive because rich people buy more.
- x Swapping impact (initial burden) and incidence (ultimate burden).
- x Saying public finance moves from income to expenditure (that is private finance).
- x Mixing up leading (forecast) and lagging (confirm) indicators.
- x Forgetting that indirect tax shifts AS left while direct tax shifts AD left.

## recall Quick recall | 214-228
- Public finance = government income and expenditure.
- Scope: expenditure, revenue, debt, financial administration.
- Public finance: expenditure first; private: income first.
- Fiscal tools: government revenue and government expenditure.
- Expansionary: cut tax, raise spending.
- Contractionary: raise tax, cut spending.
- Crowding out: government spending shrinks private sector.
- Tax functions: fiscal, allocation, regulatory, incentive.
- Smith canons: equality, certainty, convenience, economy.
- Other canons: adequacy, flexibility, simplicity, diversity.
- Direct: cannot shift; indirect: can shift.
- Progressive, proportional (flat), regressive (GST).
- Rostow: traditional, pre-takeoff, takeoff, maturity, mass consumption.
- Indicators: leading, coincident, lagging (3-12 months).
