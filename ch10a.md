## concept Purpose and general requirements | 361-362
- * Published financial statements let outsiders (owners, lenders, suppliers, customers) judge performance, position, liquidity and cash flows.
- * IAS 1 sets the basis of presentation so statements can be compared with the entity's earlier years and with other entities: overall requirements, structure guidance and minimum content.
- * Statements must present fairly (true and fair view) the position, performance and cash flows.
- Comparatives for the previous period are required (you will not be asked to prepare them).
- * Each statement must show prominently: entity name, reporting date or period, currency, and rounding level (e.g. thousands or millions).
- Names are not fixed - "balance sheet" and "income statement" are acceptable alternatives.
- def Statement of financial position :: A snapshot of assets, liabilities and equity at one date.
- def Statement of comprehensive income :: Profit or loss for the period (income earned less expenses incurred) plus other comprehensive income.

## rule Current vs non-current classification | 362-363
- * Asset is CURRENT if any one applies: realised, sold or consumed in the normal operating cycle; held for trading; expected to be realised within 12 months of the reporting date; cash or cash equivalent not restricted for at least 12 months.
- * Liability is CURRENT if any one applies: settled in the normal operating cycle; held mainly for trading; due within 12 months; no unconditional right to defer settlement for at least 12 months.
- Everything else is non-current.
- * Inventory and trade receivables are current even if cash takes more than 12 months, because they turn over within the operating cycle; likewise all trade payables are current.
- def Operating cycle :: Time from acquiring assets for processing to realising them in cash; assumed to be 12 months when it is not clearly identifiable (almost always).
- eg Five-year loan repayable in one sum at the end: non-current in years 1-4, current in year 5.
- Accrued expenses and deferred income are current liabilities.
- eg Cash = money in a current account; cash equivalent = money in a term deposit.

## rule Minimum line items | 363-366
| Statement | Minimum face items |
| * Financial position - assets | PPE, intangibles, long-term investments, inventories, trade and other receivables, cash and cash equivalents |
| * Financial position - liabilities and equity | Trade and other payables, bank loans, current tax; issued capital and reserves of the owners |
| * Comprehensive income | Revenue, finance costs, tax expense, profit or loss, each component of OCI, total comprehensive income |
- * Add extra line items whenever they help users understand position or performance.
- Sub-classification on the face or in notes: PPE by class (IAS 16), receivables into trade / related party / prepayments / other, inventories into materials / WIP / finished goods (IAS 2).
- f Total comprehensive income :: Profit or loss for the period + other comprehensive income :: OCI e.g. revaluation gains :: Profit 100 + OCI 0 = 100 :: OCI is not part of profit or loss
- * The two parts may be shown as one statement or two; if two, together they must still contain everything.
- Material items shown separately (face or notes): inventory write-down to NRV, PPE write-down to recoverable amount, PPE disposals, litigation settlements.

## compare Expenses by nature vs by function | 366-369
| Point | By nature | By function (cost of sales method) |
| * Headings | Raw materials used, staff costs, depreciation, other expenses | Cost of sales, distribution, administrative, other expenses |
| Gross profit shown? | No | Yes |
| Inventory movement | Separate line "changes in inventories of WIP and finished goods" | Built into cost of sales |
| Popularity | Varies by business type | Used by most entities in practice |
- * Inventory change line (nature method): a decrease is an expense; an increase is a negative expense.
- eg Entity Red (nature): income 14,823 + 22 = 14,845; expenses (86) + 5,565 + 4,926 + 1,533 + 2,118 = 14,056; profit before tax 789.
- eg Entity Red (function): 14,823 - 6,214 = gross profit 8,609; + 22 - 3,693 - 3,901 - 248 = 789 - same profit either way.
- tip Nature = WHAT was spent; function = WHERE in the business it was spent.

## compare Trading vs service organisations | 369-370
| Point | Trading / manufacturing | Service |
| * Main asset | Inventory | Receivables |
| Liquidity | Less cash; capital locked in inventory | Funds tied up in receivables |
| * Cost of goods sold line | Yes | No - may show cost of revenues instead |
- eg Advertising agency: revenue 400,000 - cost of revenues 250,000 = 150,000; less R&D 30,000, selling 60,000, admin 40,000 = operating profit 20,000; finance 2,000 -> profit 18,000.

## formula Cost of sales | 371
- * Cost of sales means the cost of goods SOLD (matching concept), not the cost of goods bought or made.
- f Cost of sales :: Opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory :: Purchases net of returns outwards (and, per the text, discounts received); carriage inwards is added :: 5,000 + 45,000 = 50,000 available; - 12,000 = 38,000 :: Carriage OUTWARDS is a distribution cost, not cost of sales
- je Recording closing inventory :: Dr Inventory (statement of financial position) :: Cr Cost of sales
- * Profit must be known before the statement of financial position is finished, because it forms part of equity.

## process Four-step technique for trial balance questions | 371-376
- * 1 Lay out blank formats of both statements.
- * 2 Carry every trial balance figure into the formats once - nothing missed, nothing doubled.
- * 3 Post BOTH sides of each year-end adjustment into the relevant lines.
- * 4 Sub-total and total; check assets = equity + liabilities.
- ! Trial balance items appear once; each adjustment appears twice (its double entry).
- eg ABC: cost of sales 15,000 + 304,400 - 16,500 = 302,900; gross profit 125,100.
  - eg Expenses: wages 64,000 + depreciation 14,600 (10% x 146,000) + rent 13,300 (14,000 - 700) + heat 5,400 (5,000 + 400) + bad and doubtful 1,700 (1,200 + 500) = 99,000 -> profit 26,100.
  - eg Capital 121,600 + 26,100 - 22,000 = 125,700; + payables 42,000 + accrual 400 = 168,100 = total assets.
- eg Steven Chee (Rs 000): revenue 402,200 - discounts allowed 18,000 = 384,200; purchases 250,000 - returns 15,000 - discounts received 4,800 = 230,200; cost of sales 50,000 + 230,200 - 42,000 = 238,200.
  - eg Depreciation: buildings 1.5% x 120,000 = 1,800; equipment 25% x (80,000 - 38,000) = 10,500. Allowance 2% x 38,000 = 760, increase 260.
  - eg Net profit 146,000 - 99,260 = 46,740; equity 121,300 + 46,740 - 24,000 = 144,040.

## concept Adjusted trial balance | 377-384
- * def Adjusted trial balance :: The full list of general ledger balances at period end after all adjusting entries (accruals, unearned income, depreciation, allowances, closing inventory) have been posted.
- def Adjusting entries :: Period-end journals that bring balances up to date for items that happened but were not yet recorded.
- * Flow: unadjusted trial balance -> adjusting entries -> adjusted trial balance -> financial statements.
| Aspect | Unadjusted trial balance | Adjusted trial balance |
| Timing | Period end, before adjustments | Period end, after adjustments |
| * Use | Arithmetic check only | Arithmetic check AND base for the financial statements |
| Reliability | Not fit for final reporting | Fit for final reporting |
- * Both must balance. A balanced trial balance can still hide errors; an unbalanced one proves at least one error.
- eg Receivables 100,000 with 5,000 irrecoverable -> adjusted balance 95,000.
- eg ABC worksheet: unadjusted 627,600 each side; adjustment columns 33,900 each side; adjusted 659,600 each side. Net debit-side change = -700 + 400 + 700 + 1,700 + 14,600 + 16,500 - 1,200 = 32,000.
- je Closing inventory in the worksheet :: Dr Inventory (current asset) :: Cr Cost of sales
- je Prepaid expense :: Dr Prepayments :: Cr Expense
- je Accrued expense :: Dr Expense :: Cr Accruals
- je Depreciation :: Dr Depreciation expense :: Cr Accumulated depreciation

## example Radiant Lights - adjusted trial balance (Rs m) | 382-384
- * Perpetual inventory 170: normal shortage 2 -> cost of sales; fire damage 15 -> expense; inventory 153.
- * Credit sale of 100 recorded gross despite 5% trade discount -> cut sales and debtors by 5 (sales 745).
- * Recovery of 2 on a debt written off last year was credited to debtors -> Dr debtors 2, Cr expenses 2; allowance cut by 3 to 9.
- f Unearned maintenance income :: Amount received x months not yet earned / contract months :: Annual contract to 30 April 2017 :: 12 x 4/12 = 4 unearned -> misc income 41 :: The unearned part is a current liability, not income
- f Prepaid rent :: Annual rent x months after year end / 12 :: Paid 1 October :: 24 x 9/12 = 18 prepaid :: Only 3 months belong to this year
- f Accrued loan interest :: Loan x rate x months since last payment / 12 :: Last paid 1 August :: 120 x 10% x 5/12 = 5 :: Count from the last payment date, not the loan date
- f Reducing-balance depreciation :: (Cost - accumulated depreciation) x rate :: 15% :: (270 - 150) x 15% = 18 :: Not on cost
- eg Results: gross profit 745 - 306 = 439; - 156 = 283; + 41 - 15 = net profit 309; capital 70 + 309 - 50 = 329; total assets 726.

## exam How it is tested (MCQ / numeric entry) | 362-384
- ! Classification MCQs: is a loan / receivable / accrual current or non-current at this date? Apply the four tests one by one.
- ! "Which is NOT a minimum line item / NOT part of a set of financial statements" - know the lists cold.
- ! Nature vs function: spot which heading belongs to which method; the profit figure is the same.
- ! Numeric entry asks for ONE line (cost of sales, net receivables, an expense, total assets) - work only that figure, but apply every adjustment that touches it.
- ! Memorise: cost of sales formula, unearned / prepaid time apportionment, accrued interest, RB depreciation, allowance movement.
- ! Adjusted trial balance totals: always equal; an adjustment changes two lines.

## mistakes Common mistakes | 362-384
- x Classing trade receivables or inventory as non-current because cash will take over 12 months.
- x Putting carriage outwards or returns in the wrong place in cost of sales.
- x Forgetting the second half of an adjustment (e.g. recording the accrual expense but not the liability).
- x Charging reducing-balance depreciation on cost instead of carrying amount.
- x Leaving the whole advance receipt in income instead of deferring the unearned part.
- x Finishing the statement of financial position before profit is known.

## recall Quick recall | 361-384
- IAS 1: fair presentation, comparatives, identification (name, date, currency, rounding).
- Current = operating cycle, trading, 12 months, or unrestricted cash.
- Operating cycle assumed 12 months if unclear.
- Minimum face items: PPE, intangibles, investments, inventory, receivables, cash; payables, loans, tax; capital and reserves.
- SCI minimum: revenue, finance cost, tax, profit, OCI items, total comprehensive income.
- TCI = profit + OCI.
- By nature = what; by function = where (cost of sales method, most common).
- Service firm: receivables heavy, no cost of goods sold line.
- Cost of sales = opening + purchases - closing.
- Four steps: formats, transfer once, adjust twice, total.
- Adjusted TB = all balances after adjustments; base for the statements.
- Balanced TB does not prove no errors.
