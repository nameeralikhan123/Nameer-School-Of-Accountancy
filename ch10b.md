## concept Partnership basics | 385
- * def Partnership :: Under the Partnership Act 1932, the relationship of persons who agree to share the profits of a business run by all of them or by any of them acting for all.
- def Partners / firm / firm name :: Each person is a partner; together they are the firm; the business name is the firm name.
- * Financial statements are the same as a sole trader's except for equity: each partner has a separate capital account.
- * Profit is worked out by the normal rules, then divided among partners and credited to their capital accounts.
- x Notional items (partner salaries, interest on capital) are NOT expenses - they never reduce profit for the period; they are only a way of sharing it.
- ! PSR is whatever the partners agree - it need not follow the capital ratio.
- def Profit sharing ratio (PSR) :: The agreed ratio for splitting residual profit AND loss.

## rule First-share items and default rules | 386
| Item | Effect on profit to share | Why used |
| * Partner salary | Deducted first, credited to that partner | Rewards extra responsibility without changing PSR |
| Commission / bonus | Deducted first | Links reward to individual performance |
| * Interest on capital | Deducted first | Fair when work is equal but capital is not; rate agreed |
| * Interest on drawings | ADDED to profit, charged to partner | Discourages needless withdrawals; runs from withdrawal date to year end |
- * No agreement (express or implied) -> Partnership Act 1932 applies:
  - profits and losses shared equally;
  - no interest on capital, no interest on drawings, no salaries;
  - an advance beyond agreed capital earns interest at 6% a year.
- def Profit and loss appropriation account :: A nominal account showing how the period's profit or loss is divided among partners.

## formula Appropriation statement | 386-389
- f Residual profit :: Net profit + interest on drawings - salaries - commission - interest on capital :: Residual is split in PSR :: 97,050 + 5,950 - 32,000 - 23,000 = 48,000 :: Interest on drawings is ADDED, not deducted
- f Partner's total share :: Salary + interest on capital - interest on drawings + PSR share of residual :: Per partner column :: A: 17,000 + 5,000 - 1,400 + 8,000 = 28,600 :: Column totals must equal net profit
- f Interest on drawings :: Drawing x rate x months from withdrawal to year end / 12 :: Rate agreed :: 100,000 x 12% x 6/12 = 6,000 :: Time runs to the YEAR END, not from the year start
- eg PQR: 345,000 - R's salary 30,000 = 315,000 in 4:3:2 -> P 140,000, Q 105,000, R 70,000 + 30,000 = 100,000.
- eg DEF: 95,000 - salaries 11,000 - interest 5% (5,000 + 6,000 + 3,000) = 70,000 in 3:5:2 -> D 30,000, E 41,000, F 24,000.
- eg GHI: 1,146,000 - 150,000 - 96,000 (8% of 1,200,000) = 900,000 in 3:2:1 -> G 490,000, H 382,000, I 274,000.
- * Totals check: 28,600 + 32,250 + 36,200 = 97,050 net profit.

## process Partners' capital accounts | 388-389
- * Opening capital + total share of profit - drawings = closing capital (one column per partner).
- eg ABC: A 100,000 + 28,600 - 20,000 = 108,600; B 200,000 + 32,250 - 25,000 = 207,250; C 160,000 + 36,200 - 40,000 = 156,200.
- eg XYZ: interest 6% (60,000 / 48,000 / 36,000), residual 1,800,000 in 4:3:2; X closing 1,000,000 + 860,000 - 780,000 = 1,080,000.
- je Crediting each partner's profit share :: Dr Profit and loss appropriation :: Cr Partner's capital account
- A loss share is deducted from capital in the same way.

## rule Guarantees and change of PSR | 390-391
- * Guaranteed minimum share: first share normally, ignoring the guarantee; any shortfall is made up by the OTHER partners in their own PSR.
- eg XYZ: 80,000 - X salary 20,000 = 60,000 in 2:4:6 -> Y gets 20,000 vs guarantee 32,000; shortfall 12,000 borne by X and Z 2:6 = 3,000 and 9,000. Final X 27,000, Y 32,000, Z 21,000.
- * PSR or salary changed mid-year: split the year's profit on a time basis, then appropriate each part under its own terms.
- eg DEF: 220,000 earned evenly -> 110,000 per half.
  - eg H1: half-year salaries 15,000 + 15,000; 80,000 in 3:5:2 -> D 39,000, E 40,000, F 31,000.
  - eg H2: salaries D 20,000, F 15,000; 75,000 in 1:3:1 -> D 35,000, E 45,000, F 30,000.
  - eg Year: D 74,000, E 85,000, F 61,000 = 220,000.
- ! Use HALF the annual salary for each half-year.

## example Partnership full questions | 408-413
- * AK Traders (70:30): net profit 9,350 - interest on capital 3,500 + interest on drawings 500 = 6,350 -> Adeel 4,445, Kashif 1,905.
  - eg Adeel total 2,500 - 300 + 4,445 = 6,645; closing capital 57,382 + 6,645 - 17,500 = 46,527.
  - eg Depreciation on cost: vehicles 25% x 21,400 = 5,350; equipment 20% x 9,200 = 1,840.
- * MA Enterprises (equal shares) shows a LOSS: (12,800) + 480 interest on drawings - 2,400 interest on capital = (14,720) -> (7,360) each.
  - eg Mano: 1,500 - 240 - 7,360 = (6,100); capital 50,000 - 6,100 - 6,000 drawings = 37,900.
  - ! Interest on capital is still credited even when there is a loss - it makes the loss to share bigger.
- je Unrecorded cheque paid to a supplier :: Dr Trade payables :: Cr Bank (overdraft rises)

## formula Recurring year-end adjustments | 392-407
- je Goods taken by owner (included in purchases) :: Dr Drawings :: Cr Purchases
- je Owner's personal costs paid by business (wages withdrawn, personal tax, owner's residence rent or family insurance) :: Dr Drawings :: Cr Expense account
- je Owner brings a personal asset into the business :: Dr Asset (e.g. car) :: Cr Capital
- je Business expense paid from owner's own pocket :: Dr Expense :: Cr Capital
- f Inventory write-down :: Cost - NRV, where NRV = selling price - costs to sell :: Lower of cost and NRV :: Cost 240; NRV 250 - 16 = 234 -> write-down 6; inventory 7,400 - 6 = 7,394 :: Write down only the damaged items, not all inventory
- f Allowance movement :: Required closing allowance - opening allowance :: Closing = % x receivables AFTER write-offs :: 2% x 38,000 = 760 - 500 = 260 increase :: Bad debts written off are charged in full on top
- f Asset delivered against a part advance :: Cost = advance / advance % :: Balance is a payable :: 384 / 40% = 960 cost; payable 576; RB depreciation 25% x 960 x 1/12 = 20 :: Clear the advance account to nil
- f Dishonoured cheque received net of settlement discount :: Restore receivable at gross = cheque / (1 - discount %) :: Reverse the discount allowed too :: 285 / 95% = 300 receivable; discount 15 reversed; bank down 285 :: Do not restore at the net amount
- f Accrued deposit interest :: Principal x rate x months / 12 - interest already recorded :: Rate per annum :: 1,400 x 7% = 98 for the year; 82 recorded -> accrue 16 :: Check the period the recorded interest covers
- eg Insurance: last year's prepaid 140 expires this year (expense); new annual premium 300 to 31 January 2019 -> prepaid 300 x 7/12 = 175; expense 140 + 300 - 175 = 265.

## example Numeric MCQ drills | 414-439
- f Unearned contract income :: Total contract price x months unexpired / contract months, compared with cash received :: Advance 1.8 = 50% so price 3.6 :: 3.6 x 4/9 = 1.6 earned vs 1.8 received -> 0.2 unearned; other income 15 - 0.2 = 14.8 :: Earned part is based on the FULL price, not just the advance
- f Loan with accrued interest credited to it :: Principal = balance / (1 + period rate) :: 12% a year = 3% a quarter :: 5,150 / 1.03 = 5,000 loan; 150 shown as interest payable :: Finance cost needs no change - only the classification
- eg Allowance at 6% after writing off 1 of 100: 99 x 6% = 5.94; expense = bad debts 3 + 1 + increase (5.94 - 4.5) 1.44 = 5.44; net receivables 99 - 5.94 = 93.06.
- eg Ageing allowance: 1,000 x 5% + 900 x 10% + 530 x 20% = 246; expense 230 + (246 - 220) = 256; net receivables 4,400 - 246 = 4,154.
- eg Sales recorded gross with trade discount debited separately: 353,300 - 2,432 - 4,500 discount allowed - 10,000 returns = 336,368.
- eg Photocopier: 545,000 - prepaid 75,000 x 1/3 + per-copy accrual 40,000 x 0.40 = 536,000.
- eg Supplies 90,000 unused, 30% spoiled -> asset 63,000; expense 210,000 - 63,000 = 147,000.
- eg Capital: 5,223,000 + 85,000 repairs paid personally + 960,000 vehicle + 880,000 profit - 644,000 goods withdrawn = 6,504,000.
- eg Printer rented then bought: rent 480,000 for year from 1 October; 6 months used = 240,000 expense; unused 240,000 offsets the 1,240,000 price -> payable 1,000,000; PPE +1,240,000; prepayments 3,000,000 - 480,000 = 2,520,000 (Rs).
- eg Advance-paid machine received 1 October: depreciation (12,500 - 4,630) x 15% = 1,180 + 450 x 15% x 3/12 = 17 -> 1,197; PPE net 12,950 - 5,827 = 7,123.

## exam How it is tested (MCQ / numeric entry) | 414-441
- ! Partnership MCQs: default Act rules (equal sharing, no salaries or interest, 6% on advances), effect of interest on drawings (increases profit to share), salary deducted before PSR split.
- ! "How much does Partner A get?" -> salary + PSR share of (profit - salaries): 200,000 + 800,000 x 60% = 680,000.
- ! Numeric entry usually asks for ONE line - list every adjustment touching that line, then compute.
- ! Separate owner items (drawings) from business expenses before computing any expense line.
- ! Adjusted trial balance MCQs: debits always equal credits; errors of omission and of principle are NOT revealed by it.
- ! Memorise: appropriation order, guarantee shortfall rule, time-split for PSR changes, NRV write-down, allowance movement, unearned / prepaid apportionment.
- tip Sticky-note checklist for appropriation: salaries, commission / bonus, interest on drawings, interest on capital, residual PSR, minimum guarantee, PSR change during the year.

## mistakes Common mistakes | 385-439
- x Deducting partner salaries or interest on capital as expenses in the statement of profit or loss.
- x Subtracting interest on drawings from profit instead of adding it.
- x Sharing a guarantee shortfall equally instead of in the other partners' PSR.
- x Using the full annual salary in each half of a split year.
- x Leaving owner's personal items (family insurance, residence rent, goods taken) inside business expenses.
- x Restoring a dishonoured cheque at the net amount and forgetting to reverse the discount allowed.

## recall Quick recall | 385-441
- Partnership = agreement to share profits of a business carried on by all or any acting for all.
- Separate capital account per partner; rest of the statements as for a sole trader.
- Notional items share profit - they are not expenses.
- Order: profit + interest on drawings - salaries / commission - interest on capital = residual in PSR.
- No agreement: equal shares, no salary, no interest either way, 6% on advances above agreed capital.
- Guarantee shortfall: other partners bear it in their PSR.
- PSR change mid-year: time-apportion profit, then apply each period's terms.
- Closing capital = opening + share of profit - drawings.
- Appropriation account is a nominal account.
- Goods taken by owner: Dr drawings, Cr purchases.
- Owner's asset introduced: Dr asset, Cr capital.
- Inventory at lower of cost and NRV; NRV = selling price - costs to sell.
- Allowance expense = write-offs + (closing - opening allowance).
