## concept Banks - origin and definitions | 280
- * Goldsmith origin: people left gold and silver coins with a trusted goldsmith, who noticed only a small part was ever withdrawn, began lending the rest for a return, and his receipts circulated as money - this grew into formal banking.
- * def Bank :: A government-licensed financial institution that accepts deposits and puts those funds to work in loans and securities - in short, it borrows and lends money.
- * def Financial intermediary :: An institution standing between savers and borrowers, so savers fund borrowers indirectly through it (e.g. a pension fund).
- def Credit :: An agreement under which a borrower gets something of value now and pays for it later, usually with interest.
- def Credit creation :: The way commercial banks expand money supply by creating new deposits in borrowers' names out of an initial deposit.

## process Functions of commercial banks | 280
- * Receiving deposits - savers place surplus funds to earn interest; deposits are a liability of the bank.
- * Advancing loans - funds are lent at interest for household or business needs.
- * Discounting bills of exchange - a seller holding a buyer's bill can get cash from the bank before maturity, less a deduction for early payment.
- Lockers - safe custody of jewellery and documents for a nominal fee.
- * Credit creation - generating further loanable funds from deposits.
- ! Customers' deposits are a LIABILITY of the bank; balances at the central bank, money at call and advances are assets.

## formula Credit creation and the money multiplier | 279-297
- * f Credit (money) multiplier :: 1 / reserve ratio :: Reserve ratio = share of deposits banks must keep as reserves :: Reserve ratio 40% -> multiplier 1 / 0.40 = 2.5 :: The multiplier is the reciprocal of the reserve ratio, not the ratio itself
- * f Total deposits created :: Initial deposit x 1 / reserve ratio :: Initial deposit = fresh cash placed with the first bank :: Rs 10,000 x 1 / 0.40 = Rs 25,000 :: Rs 4,000 (10,000 x 40%) is only the first-round reserve, not the outcome
- * Higher reserve requirement -> slower credit creation; reserve requirement and credit creation move inversely.
- In an expansion, extra reserves lent by the first bank become deposits of second-generation banks, which lend again - a multiplied effect.

## process Central bank - nature and functions | 281-282
- * def Central bank :: The state institution that banks for commercial banks and the government, carries out state policy, and alone issues and manages the circulation of currency.
- Called the father of the banking system or watchdog of the monetary system; it works as a non-profit body aiming at sustainable growth.
- * Often trusted more than government to meet economic objectives because it is not seeking re-election.

- * Note issue - sole authority to issue uniform currency and manage its supply.
  - Fixed fiduciary system - notes up to a set limit need no backing; anything above the limit needs 100% reserves.
  - * Proportional reserve system - flexible, most widely used; notes are backed by a set percentage of gold, silver or foreign currency (30% - 40% in Pakistan).
  - f Reserve needed for note issue :: Notes to issue x reserve requirement :: Reserve requirement = required backing percentage :: Rs 100 million x 30% = Rs 30 million of gold / silver :: Under the fixed fiduciary system the 100% backing applies only above the limit
- * Banker to the government - advice and funding for projects.
- * Banker to banks - holds their cash reserves, building confidence and a buffer against shocks; also counsels banks in difficulty.
- * Lender of last resort - funds banks that cannot raise money elsewhere, adding liquidity and protecting savers.
- Exchange-rate control - uses foreign currency and gold reserves to manage exchange rates and the balance of payments.
- Clearing agent - banks settle with each other through their accounts at the central bank, reducing cash transfers.
- Custodian of monetary reserves - gold and foreign currency.
- ! Fiscal policy and credit creation are NOT central bank functions.

## compare Instruments of monetary policy | 282-284
| Instrument | Type | How it works |
| * Bank rate (discount rate) | Quantitative | Rate at which the central bank rediscounts bills, i.e. lends to banks; raising it lifts borrowing costs and market rates |
| * Open market operations | Quantitative | Buying or selling government securities to change commercial banks' reserves |
| * Reserve requirement | Quantitative | Changing the reserves banks must hold at the central bank; effect is magnified by the multiplier |
| Credit rationing | Quantitative | A ceiling on how much each bank may lend |
| Marginal requirement | Qualitative | Gap between the security value demanded and the loan given; widening it restricts credit |
| * Moral persuasion | Qualitative | Talks and persuasion to stop banks acting against central bank aims; hard to measure |
| Direct action | Qualitative | Last-resort punishment of a non-cooperating bank: fines, refusing discounting, a penal rate above bank rate |
| Special deposit | Qualitative | Short-term deposits from banks at above-market rates, draining money from the market |
| Prudential control | Qualitative | Regulations on credit volume |
- tip The "big three" quantitative tools: bank rate, OMOs, reserve ratio. Anything that relies on persuasion or punishment is qualitative.
- ! Moral persuasion is NOT a quantitative control - a favourite MCQ.

## process Transmission chains (tight money) | 282-283
- * Higher bank rate -> dearer borrowing for banks -> fewer loanable funds -> tight money -> higher interest rates -> AD falls -> inflation controlled.
- * OMO sale of securities (e.g. Rs 1 billion of bonds) -> bought by banks / dealers -> banks' reserves at the central bank fall -> deposits shrink by the money multiplier -> tight money -> higher rates -> AD falls.
- * Higher reserve ratio -> banks can lend less -> tight money -> higher rates -> less consumption and investment -> AD (C + I + G + (X - M)) shifts left -> lower output, employment and inflation.
- eg Government bonds bought by private investors with cheques on their banks cut those banks' cash, so the banks must cut lending to keep their cash-to-loan ratio.

## compare Objectives of monetary policy and their conflicts | 284
- * Objectives: low and steady inflation; economic growth (per capita income); exchange-rate stability; full employment; credit control (with fair access for the vulnerable); correcting the current account deficit (by varying interest rates, especially on bonds).
| Conflict | Why the objectives clash |
| * Price stability vs full employment | Raising AD to create jobs pushes up inflation |
| Economic growth vs exchange-rate stability | Managing the exchange rate to boost exports undermines its stability |
| Economic growth vs credit control | Expanding credit spurs growth but raises default risk |
- ! The objectives are described as conflicting - all cannot be met at once.

## compare Contractionary vs expansionary monetary policy | 285-286
| Point | Contractionary (anti-inflationary, tight) | Expansionary (anti-deflationary, easy) |
| * When used | To cut demand and inflation | To stimulate demand when output is low |
| Tools | Raise interest / bank rate, raise reserve requirement, sell securities | Buy government bonds from banks, cut rates, lower reserve ratio |
| Bank response | Fewer loans or dearer variable-rate loans | Extra reserves lent out, multiplied through later banks |
| Interest rate | Rises | Market rate falls |
| * Effect on AD | Consumption and investment fall; AD left; output Y1 to Y2 and price P1 to P2 both fall | Consumption and investment rise; AD right; output and price level rise |
- ! To fight a recession: BUY securities and LOWER the discount rate.
- ! Raising the ATM limit is not a contractionary tool.

## concept Limitations of monetary policy | 287
- * Non-monetary sector - barter in rural areas of developing countries keeps people outside banks, so policy does not reach them.
- * Non-bank financial institutions lend outside central bank supervision.
- High liquidity - agents with liquid assets can create their own liquidity and blunt a squeeze.
- * Time lags - effects arrive late, so the central bank must forecast, and uncertainty may make it wrong.
- * Poor co-ordination with fiscal policy (government) can undermine the outcome.

## concept Role of the State Bank of Pakistan | 288
- * SBP prepares monetary policy and oversees the economic, banking and financial systems; controls foreign reserves, the currency rate and currency supply.
- Set up institutions: NBP (largest commercial bank and SBP's agent for government treasury work); ZTB for agriculture and IDBP for industry, both giving soft loans.
- Promotes Islamic banking - commercial banks told to open separate counters for Islamic deposits.
- Supports exports (easy remittances, incentives agreed with government) and discourages unnecessary imports, especially luxuries.
- * Controls inflation through open market operations, interest-rate setting, exchange-rate fixing and control of bank lending.

## exam How this is tested in the CBE | 280-297
- ! Numeric entry: deposits created = initial deposit / reserve ratio (Rs 10,000 at 40% -> Rs 25,000); note-issue reserve = notes x reserve %.
- ! Classify instruments as quantitative (bank rate, OMO, reserve ratio, credit rationing) or qualitative (margins, moral persuasion, direct action).
- ! Direction questions: tight money = sell securities, raise bank rate, raise reserve ratio; easy money = the opposite. "All of the above" options are common.
- ! Balance-sheet traps: deposits / checking accounts are liabilities; reserves, loans and investments are assets.
- ! Real rate of interest = nominal rate adjusted for inflation.
- ! Fill-in: higher interest rates make BORROWING dearer, returns on INVESTMENT less attractive, and a stronger currency discourages EXPORTS.
- ! Intermediate targets are the market rate of interest and money supply; growth and inflation are final objectives.
- ! The self-test also asks the SBP's authorised capital at establishment (answer key: PKR 30 million).

## mistakes Common traps | 280-288
- x Treating the reserve ratio, not its reciprocal, as the multiplier.
- x Listing fiscal policy as a central bank function.
- x Classifying moral persuasion as a quantitative control.
- x Saying a central bank SELLS securities to fight recession.
- x Forgetting that the fixed fiduciary 100% backing applies only above the limit.
- x Thinking all monetary policy objectives can be met together.

## recall Quick recall | 280-289
- Banking grew from goldsmith receipts and lending.
- Bank = licensed deposit-taker that lends and invests.
- Commercial bank functions: deposits, loans, bill discounting, lockers, credit creation.
- Money multiplier = 1 / reserve ratio.
- Central bank: note issue, banker to government and banks, lender of last resort, exchange control, clearing, custodian.
- Note issue systems: fixed fiduciary, proportional reserve (30-40% in Pakistan).
- Monetary policy = central bank control of money supply and credit via interest rates.
- Quantitative: bank rate, OMO, reserve ratio, credit rationing.
- Qualitative: margins, moral persuasion, direct action, special deposits, prudential rules.
- Tight money: higher rates, AD left, inflation down.
- Easy money: buy bonds, rates down, AD right.
- Conflicts: prices vs jobs, growth vs exchange rate, growth vs credit control.
- Limits: non-monetary sector, NBFIs, liquidity, time lags, poor co-ordination.
- SBP: monetary policy, reserves, NBP / ZTB / IDBP, Islamic banking, OMOs.
